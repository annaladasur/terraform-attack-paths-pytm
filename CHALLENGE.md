# Apply the method in your own context

Choose a scope and pace that fit your service and risk policy. This guide offers a reusable process, not an enrollment program. There are no audience reporting deadlines, commitment forms or promises to publish aggregate results. The historical filename is retained for existing links.

1. **Scope a relevant change.** Identify assets, dependencies, identities, entry/exit points and trust boundaries. Include the review pipeline as well as the proposed workload.
2. **Trace a plausible threat.** Connect source evidence to a concrete failure path. Use STRIDE to prompt coverage and mark assumptions and unsupported scope.
3. **Select an enforcing control.** Name its owner, permitted behavior, forbidden behavior and remaining risk. Input access, decision authority and publication are separate permissions.
4. **Verify the control.** Test the denied operation and the necessary authorized operation. Bind evidence to the reviewed version. A model replay does not establish deployed behavior.
5. **Decide under your policy.** Routine work inside explicitly approved scope may automate. Define protected review floors and authenticated authority for escalation. Hard blocks remain blocked.
6. **Reassess when evidence changes.** Compare decision quality, misses, full latency, review effort, cost and service impact with your own baseline. Continue, change or stop as appropriate.

Use your existing tools where they fit. The [optional kit](kit/README.md), replay and labs illustrate the approach; adopting this prototype is not required. Keep plans, evidence and decisions within your own information-handling policy. Nothing needs to be sent to the speaker.

## If you use a risk classifier

The talk recommends policy-bounded zero/one/two-person routing, not review of every routine change. Classification is fallible and influences security. Adjudicate feedback from incidents, overrides and sampled automatic routes. Evaluate regression and held-out cases, including false-low routing, before shadow/canary deployment and independently approved promotion. Version and protect the classifier and policy, monitor drift and suspend automation when needed. Approval clicks alone are not ground truth. The classifier cannot expand its own authority.

This is recommended architecture, not a demonstrated autonomous approval classifier or measured reinforcement-learning system. The prototype demonstrates deterministic path/gate checks and bounded annotations. The conference speaker package documents control evidence and remaining limits.
